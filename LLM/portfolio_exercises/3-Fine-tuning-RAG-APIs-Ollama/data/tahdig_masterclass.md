# Masterclass on Persian Tahdig (The Crispy Rice Crust)

Tahdig (literally "bottom of the pot") is the prized golden crust formed at the base of the Persian rice pot (dig). Achieving glass-shattering crispness without scorching requires mastering thermodynamic balance, fat ratios, and steaming pressure.

## 1. Golden Potato Tahdig (Tahdig-e Sibzamini)
The most beloved variety, featuring thin, translucent-to-crispy saffron-infused potato medallions.

* **Potato Slicing Thickness:** Exactly 3 mm to 4 mm thickness. Slicing too thin causes potatoes to burn before the rice steams; slicing too thick leads to soggy centers.
* **Pre-soaking Technique:** Soak potato slices in cold salted water for 15 minutes, then pat completely dry on paper towels to remove surface starch.
* **The Layering Protocol:**
  - Heat 3 tablespoons neutral oil and 1 tablespoon bloomed saffron water over medium heat until shimmering.
  - Arrange potato slices in a single, slightly overlapping concentric circle.
  - Cook potatoes undisturbed for 3 minutes before packing parboiled rice on top.
* **Pot Selection:** Heavy-bottom non-stick cast aluminum or Teflon pot. Stainless steel will cause sticking unless seasoned with extreme precision.

## 2. Yogurt & Saffron Tahdig (Tahdig-e Masty)
A rich, savory cake-like crust commonly prepared for Tahchin.

* **Base Emulsion Ratio:**
  - 1 cup full-fat strained Greek yogurt or labneh
  - 2 egg yolks (never whole eggs; egg whites make tahdig rubbery)
  - 3 tablespoons brewed saffron
  - 3 tablespoons melted clarified butter (ghee) or vegetable oil
* **Cooking Time:**
  - 10 minutes on medium heat to set the egg protein matrix.
  - 50 to 60 minutes on lowest heat with a damkoni (cloth-wrapped pot lid) to steam through.
